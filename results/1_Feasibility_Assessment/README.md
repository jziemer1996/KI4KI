#### 1) Feasibility Assessment of MT-InSAR and GMS for Dam Monitoring

Our findings collectively demonstrate that Ground Motion Service (GMS) InSAR data, particularly from the German Ground Motion Service (BBD), can feasibly support dam monitoring, provided that site-specific and methodological constraints are carefully assessed in advance. Comparisons with in situ measurements at gravity dams in Germany consistently show fair to good agreement for long-term deformation trends, with reported coefficients of determination ranging from R² ≈ 0.5 up to 0.8. These results confirm that GMS-based PSI data are capable of capturing millimeter-scale deformation signals relevant for dam safety assessments.  

However, the feasibility of using GMS data strongly depends on dam orientation, dam type, radar viewing geometry, topography, observation period, and update frequency. Dams with unfavorable alignment relative to the satellite look direction — such as east–west–oriented structures — exhibit reduced line-of-sight (LOS) sensitivity to radial deformations, leading to increased errors (RMSE between 6 mm and 43 mm) compared to pendulum measurements. Statistical analyses show that for angular differences of up to 20° between the dam’s radial direction and the satellite look direction, only 50–70% of the true radial deformation is captured in the LOS signal, depending on incidence angle. Feasibility assessments using indices such as the CR-Index (radar visibility with regard to topography, dam type and material properties) and radial sensitivity metrics are therefore essential prior to operational use.  

Across multiple sites and observation periods, BBD data proved particularly valuable for monitoring long-term deformation trends, achieving correlation coefficients of up to r ≈ 0.7–0.8, while its suitability for short-term deformation detection remains limited due to the annual update cycle. Interestingly, extending the observation period from 2015–2020 to 2015–2021 resulted in a reduction of PS points by up to 60%, but simultaneously improved agreement with in situ data, indicating higher time-series quality. Overall, the studies conclude that GMS-based MT-InSAR data are a robust complementary tool for dam monitoring. For implementation into operational monitoring programs, greater effort must be spent on near-real-time updates of BBD datasets.
